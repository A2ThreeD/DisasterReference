# Editorial method

## Intake and verification

1. Review each submitted project or site. Capture its relevant resource recommendations and the exact locations in which they appear.
2. Match each candidate to the original publisher, edition, and verified format. Collapse mirrors and renamed copies of the same edition into a single resource record. Track applicable jurisdiction, audience, and rights.
3. Record each piece of evidence with a locator and check date. A specific document recommendation, support for the general topic, availability in a collection, and a published concern each have their own evidence type.
4. Trace overlapping lists to their origin. Assign copied or jointly maintained recommendations to the same independence group. Where no lineage is established, leave the group blank until reviewed.

## Selecting the core list

Start with references that address common household needs across multiple disasters. Favor accessible language, practical use without connectivity, identifiable authorship, current guidance, and manageable size. For first aid, water safety, and other high-consequence topics, verify guidance against the relevant authority and document the geographic and audience limits.

A proposed core resource needs two confirmed independent groups explicitly recommending that work, plus maintainer review. Topic agreement helps prioritize research and identify gaps. It does not count as endorsement of a specific document. Edition uncertainty, substantive disagreement, and superseded guidance need a documented resolution before promotion.

Aim for three to five core documents per category as the collection matures. Publish fewer where that covers the task or where stronger candidates still need review. Lead with the best concise guide for each distinct task. Link to the exact chapter, page, or checklist when a reader needs only part of a document. Put comprehensive manuals, collections, and ZIM archives after the first readings.

Use the situational tier for reviewed references serving specific hazards, climates, jurisdictions, equipment, or skill levels. Record why each reviewed resource belongs in its tier. A category can have a gap while evidence is gathered. Prefer the smallest set that gives readers useful coverage.

## Testing access

For each category, try finding a specific answer from a phone and an e-ink reader while offline. A task label should lead to the relevant reading in at most two selections from the index. Check that search finds the guide using everyday terms. Open the saved copy with networking off and verify that the cited page, diagrams, tables, and warnings are present wherever they carry meaning. Keep archive search available for deeper questions.

## Keeping it current

Revisit links and editions before each published snapshot. Retain retired IDs and a replacement pointer where available. When an original URL stops working, record the issue and verify any replacement against the publisher and edition. If the guidance changes materially, review the successor as a new candidate.
